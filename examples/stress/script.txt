# "The Lighthouse Radio": an invented 40-line test script for the stress test.
Nobody remembers when the lighthouse stopped working.
The lamp went dark one winter and nobody came to fix it.
But every night at nine, a radio still plays inside.
My grandfather says it was the keeper's radio.
He says the keeper left in a hurry and never came back.
I went to see for myself last Tuesday.

The path to the lighthouse is narrow and full of stones.
The wind pushes you toward the water the whole way.
The door was locked, but a window on the side was open.
Inside it smelled of salt, oil and old paper.
There were maps on the walls and a kettle on the stove.
The stairs went up in a tight spiral, one hundred and twelve steps.
I counted every one of them on the way up.

At the top, the big lamp was covered in dust.
Next to it sat a small wooden radio with a brass dial.
It was switched off, and the plug was not connected.
I waited until nine o'clock, sitting on the floor.
At nine exactly, the radio began to play.
It was a slow song, an old one, with a piano.
Then a voice came on and read the weather for the coast.
Light winds from the west, it said, and calm seas tonight.
I looked outside and the sea was wild and grey.

The next night I came back with my grandfather.
He did not want to climb the stairs, so I helped him.
At nine the radio played the same slow song.
My grandfather sat very still and closed his eyes.
He told me he had danced to that song once, a long time ago.
The voice came on again and read the weather.
This time it said a storm was coming from the north.
We walked home quickly, and the rain started at the door.

In the morning the harbour was full of broken boats.
Every fisherman who had stayed home was safe.
My grandfather said the radio had warned us.
I think the radio warns anyone who is willing to listen.
Now we climb the stairs together every Friday.
We bring tea in a flask and sit by the dusty lamp.
Sometimes the song is the same, and sometimes it is new.
The weather is always right, even when the sky says otherwise.
Nobody else believes us, and that is fine.
The lighthouse is dark, but it still looks after the coast.
